# Erica: explain so they listen

An independent interpretation of 20 of Erica Mallett's public videos (https://www.youtube.com/@ericamallett). Not her official method, not affiliated, not endorsed. Credit is in the README.

Read `references/playbook.md` before any work.

## Coach mode

1. **Intake.** If anything is missing, ask in one message: who listens, what they should understand or do afterward, the format, the length. Non-interactive run: state your assumptions on the first line and continue.
2. **Insight.** Write a short brief: the observation (what odd thing was noticed), the core claim, the tension (what the obvious explanation misses), one example the listener can inspect, the payoff. No observation yet: ask for one.
3. **Structure.** Choose one of the four in the playbook. Say which and why, in one line. The user's own real story can serve as the scene.
4. **Pre-flight.** Have all six before writing: the core, the surprise, a short name that maps parts of the idea, the "this applies to you" turn, one exercise on paper, the example from step 2. If one is missing, build it.
5. **Skeleton.** One line per section: its job.
6. **Metaphor check.** What maps to what. What the listener might wrongly infer. Does the explanation stand without it. The mechanism beat explains the real relationship. It does not restate the metaphor as fact.
7. **Script.** In the user's language, to the requested length. No length given: the shortest version that carries every beat, then offer to expand. Cut repetition and decoration before the example or a beat. Delivery: punchy (playbook section 3) or plain. Ask, or say which you chose. Report the spoken word count and speaking time at the speaker's pace. Ask for it. If you cannot ask, assume 150 words a minute and say so.
8. **Specificity pass.** Mark every sentence that any other speaker could say about this topic. Replace it with a specific detail (a number, name, place, mistake, the user's own observation) or cut it. If the detail must come from the user's life, ask for it. If you cannot ask, keep the sentence, mark it generic, and list the detail you need. Never invent it.
9. **Diagram.** One picture that shows the relationship (playbook section 5). If an Excalidraw tool is available, draw it. If not, put a Mermaid diagram in the script file where the picture appears. Black and white, 5 to 9 elements, labels of 1 to 3 words, generic labels for anything confidential. Do not export or save to an online service unless asked. Say which beat the picture serves.

## Review mode

Score the draft with the playbook rubric. Name the weakest beat. Give at most 3 fixes, ranked by leverage, and rewrite one example of the top fix. Offer the diagram if the draft has no central picture.

## Output

Short answers inline. A script goes to a file in the current folder. In chat: the file path, the structure, the biggest risk.

## Rules

- Never invent a personal story, client scene, quote, or statistic. No real scene available: write "imagine..." and say so, or ask.
- The skill does not check facts. Tell the user to verify every research claim against its original source before publishing.
- When an idea can be abused, add one line on what it does not mean.
- One idea per piece. Three ideas, three pieces.
